⚡ Sprints
6 × 40 m for referees, 5 × 30 m for ARs. Miss one? One make-up sprint. Miss two, you’re done.
 
🔄 Intervals
10 laps: run 75 m, walk 25 m. First missed zone is a caution, the second is a fail.
 
🔀 CODA
Change-of-direction test for assistant referees. You get two tries.

Test day: arrive 35 minutes early with running shoes and water. A pass counts for one certification year.

05 · SAFETY ON THE FIELD

Protect the game.
Protect each other.

Three things every Arizona ref should know cold. One quick refresher on each.

🟥 Red cards: report within 8 hours

File every red card and supplemental report at redcards.azref.com within 8 hours of scheduled kickoff. Pull your game from Assignr with your email, tap the facts, done. Most take about 2 minutes. Supplementals cover discriminatory language, serious injuries, abandoned matches, and crowd or field problems. Exceptions: MLS NEXT and games assigned by U.S. Soccer use their own systems.

🚫 Discriminatory & harmful language
We never ignore it if we hear it. We never ignore an allegation.
Heard it
Red card, every time. Tell that team’s coach why. Report it.
 
Told about it
Stop and address it. Meet both coaches. Follow all 8 steps.
 
Spectator
Stop play until they’re removed. Report it.

Missed the October 15 action items? Watch the 5-minute video and run the Practice Report at redcards.azref.com this weekend.

🛡️ Referee abuse: report it, every time
When: any time a referee is mistreated. Insults, intimidation, threats, or physical contact. Before the match, during, after the final whistle, in the parking lot, or online.
Who: anyone. The ref, an AR, an assignor, a parent, a coach, a spectator. If you saw it, you can report it.
How: go to go.azref.com/abuse. Add the match details, who was involved, and what happened in order, with exact words if you can. Attach up to five photos, videos or documents.
Also: it doesn’t replace your match report. File that too. Only ASRA admins can see referees’ names.

Anyone in danger? Call 911 first. Report after.

Plus 14 refs recommended to the State Referee Committee. One thing we want to fix: those 90 first-season refs are only about 1 in 6 of our new referees. If you’re in your first season and haven’t had a visit yet, ask for one (button below).

What mentors keep writing down
 Stay with the offside line. The most common note by far. Track the second-to-last defender, and follow the ball all the way to the goal line, even when it seems obvious.
 Keep your feet moving. Get ahead of play on fast breaks instead of chasing it, and move your feet rather than leaning on tight calls.
 Trust your whistle. A confident whistle tone and calm, clear talks with coaches sell the decisions you’re already getting right.

And the good stuff they keep writing: looks the part, great work rate and excellent mechanics. Keep it up.

“Antonio looked so happy to be out at the fields. Smile on his face the whole time I watched him.”
A mentor’s note on a first-season ref, who’s now ready to move up an age group. That’s the job, folks.

Huge thanks to all 19 of our volunteer mentors, and a special shout-out to Mitchell C., who’s out front with 29 visits this season.
