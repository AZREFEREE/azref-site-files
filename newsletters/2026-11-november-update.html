04 · REPORT WRITING

Just the facts.
(Save the poetry.)

The final whistle isn’t the end of the job. It’s the end of the playing time. Your job is done when your reports are in and accepted. And skipping one is never an option: every match report gets filled out in full, correctly, for every match, and every send-off gets a written report. Every time.

Two new training videos

A good report starts before kickoff and isn’t done until it’s accepted. These two cover both ends. Watch them before your next match.

Red card reports →
How to file your red card and supplemental reports, start to finish.
 
Game cards & check-in →
Checking players in before kickoff and filling out the game card right.
Why it matters

If you don’t document it, it didn’t happen. A lazy report doesn’t save you time. It hands the same coach or player to the next referee, and that referee might be a 15-year-old in their first season.

🙈 Vague = a free pass ✅ Facts = can’t be argued
“Coach was mean and aggressive.” “The coach sprinted 8 yards from the technical area onto the field, pointed his finger in my face and told me I was an awful referee.”
“Player used abusive language.” “#20 shouted ‘You’re a cheat, how much did they pay you?’ loud enough for spectators to hear.”
“She intimidated me.” “#7 pointed her finger in my face and refused to step back.”
Result: the league can’t enforce a thing. Result: automatic suspension. The behavior stops.
Every send-off report answers six questions
When: the minute it happened.
Who: player number, name and team.
What: the offense in the Law’s words (serious foul play, violent conduct, second caution) and that the red card was shown.
Restart: how play restarted: direct free kick, penalty kick, and so on.
Exit: how the player left the field.
Anything else: any additional facts after the send-off. Facts only.
SAMPLE REPORT · SERIOUS FOUL PLAY
“In the 65th minute, #2 Melissa Beck of the Flying Sand Hornets made a straight-leg, lunging, two-footed tackle, and the bottom of her cleats contacted the ankle of #22 Joel Votaw of the Little Angels, using excessive force that endangered the safety of her opponent. She was shown the red card. Play was restarted with a direct free kick to the opposing team. The player left the field without issue.”

No adjectives. No opinions. No “so I told him to stop or else.” Just what happened, in order. Keep it accurate, keep it simple.

05 · YOUR CLIPS

Send us the good, the bad
and the ugly.

The best training clips come from Arizona fields, and that means yours. Lots of club games are filmed now (Veo, Hudl, Trace). After your match, find the video, spot the moment worth a second look, and send it in. It might become the next What’s Your Call or Clip Review for referees across the state.

Find it: ask the team manager, or check the club’s Veo, Hudl or Trace page for your match.
Send it: at go.azref.com/submit. Paste a link or upload the video (up to 2 GB, up to 3 files; a second angle is welcome).
Point us to it: a whole-game link is fine. Tell us the time in the video and what happens. We trim it, so you don’t have to.
Privacy: we don’t name players, and we never name referees under 18. Every clip lands in the ROC, and we watch every one.

Yes, send the ones that didn’t go your way. We’ve all got a clip we’d rather forget, and those teach the most.

What’s Your Call: everyone plays

When a What’s Your Call lands in your inbox, we expect every Arizona referee to play: watch the clip, make your call, then read the answer and the Law behind it. It takes two minutes. Same goes for Clip Review: watch it and steal what the best refs do. Missed the commercial? The Call-O-Matic 3000 is back by popular demand.

They come from admin@azref.com, so check your Promotions tab if you don’t see them. Operators are standing by.

06 · REFEREE SERVICES

Reffing out of state?
Tell us first.

Heading to a tournament in another state? Let us know before you go. Lots of events (and the other state’s referee office) also want a Letter of Good Standing, which proves your license is current. Both live at go.azref.com, and each takes about a minute.

✈️ Refereeing Out Of State
Tell us the event, the state, your dates and who assigned you. Your SRA reviews it and emails you when it’s approved.
 
📄 Letter Of Good Standing
Tell us who it goes to (usually that state’s referee administrator, the tournament assignor, or both). Each person gets their own letter, and you’re copied.

Also at go.azref.com: request a mentor, replace a lost badge ($5), sort out your Assignr account, ask for an evaluation, sign up for a fitness test and more. Bookmark it.
